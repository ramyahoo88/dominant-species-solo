# Paper rivals

Table-playable rules for the rival animals (two to five), so Dominant Species can be played solo without the app. **The rulebook and printable cards are in [`paper/`](paper/)**: `Paper_Rivals_Rulebook.pdf` (also `.md`, `.html`) and `Paper_Rivals_Cards.pdf` (48 cut-out cards). They are generated by `python3 source/paper/printables.py --pdf` from `source/paper/RULES.md` and the app's decks. A shareable copy is mirrored in the Claude Doc [Dominant Species — Paper Rivals](https://claude.ai/artifact/DfXqXU6D3HcUDfh8isLRUr). Since v0.3.6 the app can also run these rules: choose **Paper rules** and a level on the setup screen.

- Rules as code: [`source/paper/paper.js`](source/paper/paper.js), shipped in the app build and inactive unless a game turns it on. Every decision uses only what a person can count at the table.
- One version only (November 2026): flat element matching, a fixed Dominance Card list, always the card's reverse (No) face, simple Adaptation, full Speciation, Migration without rank-gain checks (it still avoids losing a rank where it leaves), Glaciation without the tundra bonus, Barren Earth, and **Block the leader** (deny the leader's Domination). The earlier detailed rules (card checks, card scoring, rank checks) were retired; `--paper-options full` restores them only to reproduce old results.
- Benchmark: `node source/benchmark.cjs --base main --candidate worktree --paper N [--bots 2..5]` plays the app's rivals (base) against paper rivals with N extra action pawns (candidate) on the same seeds: `--paper=-1` Easy, `--paper 0` Standard, `--paper 1` Hard, `--paper 2` Expert (v0.4.0 levels). The tuner (`--evaluate --modes=app,-1,0,1,2`) uses the same names and reports `extraPawns` with each row.

## Difficulty (v0.4.0, seed block 200000, 360 games per cell; scripted player's win rate, lower is tougher)

v0.4.0 adds the **rival tie-break**: when only rivals tie for the most matches on a tile, the rival higher in the food chain is dominant. Flat matching made rivals tie constantly, and a tie leaves a tile with no dominant animal. At game end paper rivals dominated 2.6 tiles each, against 6.0 for the app's rivals, which handed the player cards, Ice Age points and control of the board. With the tie-break the rivals are about one pawn stronger, so every level is one pawn lower: Easy −1, Standard +0, Hard +1, Expert +2.

| Rivals (even game) | Easy −1 | Standard +0 | Hard +1 | Expert +2 | App |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 (33%) | 48% | 30% | 20% | 16% | 20% |
| 3 (25%) | 36% | 26% | 11% | 8% | 15% |
| 4 (20%) | 31% | 17% | 7% | 6% | 9% |
| 5 (17%) | 30% | 11% | 5% | 3% | 8% |

No game stalled. Games started before v0.4.0 keep their rules and pawn counts.

**Strategy balance.**
- Chasing Dominance Cards is still the strongest simple strategy: at Standard it won 44%, 38%, 19% and 15% (2–5 rivals), against 2–25% for the other two.
- The self-tuned test player (`source/benchmark/tune.cjs`) found the bigger hole: fighting for turn order and the Domination spaces.
  - Against v0.3.9 rivals, the first tuned player beat the card-chaser by +40 points at +1 pawn (now Hard), where the app's rivals allowed +20.
  - With the tie-break, the player re-tuned against the new rule (checkpoint v3, `source/benchmark/tuned_weights_v3.json`) was measured twice:
    - player-bot held-out run (1,800 tables, seed 330000): at Hard 48% against 18% for the card-chaser (+30); App +28; Standard +39. Without the tie-break, +51 at Hard.
    - GPT's v0.4.0 audit (240 matched setups per cell, seeds 732000–735059): Hard +35, App +25, Standard +44.
  - So at Hard, v3's margin is 2–10 points above its margin against the app's rivals. A simple habit (Initiative whenever not first) adds about 5 points at Standard.
  - Its edge still comes from Initiative (about 10 points) and Domination weights (about 7). Details: `source/benchmark/TUNED_PLAYER.md` (player-bot branch).
- Counters tried and dropped:
  - Holding Initiative against you backfired: rivals waste a pawn each turn.
  - Blocking with more Domination pawns just made rivals tougher for everyone.

## History

### Quick mode and two-player games (earlier tests)

- **Quick mode** (optional, 6 pawns) cuts the rivals' upkeep. It uses flat element matching (printed count plus added elements, capped by the disks on the tile) and a fixed card list instead of card scoring. On seed 150000 the scripted player won 30% (107/360), against 19% (70/360) against the app's rivals on the same seeds. On the tuning seeds it was 34%. Quick-mode difficulty is set by pawns: on seed 150000 the scripted player won 48% at 5 pawns (`--paper=-1`), 30% at 6 and 25% at 7 (`--paper 1`); 7 pawns is within noise of the app's rivals. Run it with `--paper 0 --paper-options flatMatch,cardList`.
- **Two players plus a rival:** the rules work unchanged with two people and one rival, because "leader" means whichever opponent leads on VP.
- **Rejected at 6 pawns** (seed 6200, 360 games each; Standard was 49%):
  - a fixed-position Dominance Card pick: 64%
  - one generic effect for every card: 82%
  - a center-out clockwise tie-breaker: 57%
  - the card list with real matching: 56%

### Three to five rivals (earlier tests)

Use the base game's pawns and cubes for the player count, and add difficulty pawns on top (+0 Standard, +1 Hard, +2 Expert; quick mode −1/+0/+1). Results on seed block 160000, 360 games per cell; an even game is 25%, 20% and 17%:

| Rivals | App | Full +0 | Full +1 | Full +2 | Quick +0 | Quick +1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 12% | 47% | 28% | – | 30% | 19% |
| 4 | 9% | 41% | 29% | 18% | 27% | 21% |
| 5 | 9% | 39% | 19% | 13% | 25% | 16% |

Quick mode now includes five cuts (always the "no" face, simple Adaptation, full Speciation, Migration without rank-gain checks (it still avoids losing a rank where it leaves), Glaciation without the tundra bonus). Single-cut results on seed 6200 (quick base 34%): 31%, 33%, 32%, 35%, 36%. Combined across 2–5 rivals they average the same as the two-change version. Cuts that weakened the rivals and were dropped: "yes" face 39%, leftmost space 38%, simple Competition 38%, simple card effects 45%, no Wanderlust element 51%, no Domination-first 67%. Every cut weakened the full rules (52–91% against 49%).

## Results (Claude, November 14, 2026)

Share of games won by the fixed scripted player; lower means tougher rivals. 360 games per cell (60 matchups × 3 strategies × 2 repeats), on seed blocks not used for tuning.

| Rivals | Seeds 120000 | Seeds 130000 | Seeds 140000 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| App v0.3.4 | 23% | 22% | 22% |
| Paper, Standard | 52% | 52% | not run |
| Paper, Hard | 28% | 35% | 37% |
| Paper, Expert | 24% | 30% | 28% |

The 120000 runs used the rules just before the final change to the countable `elements` card condition (Standard moved from 52% to 49% on the tuning seeds with that change). The 130000 and 140000 runs used `source/benchmark.cjs --paper` with the final rules.

**How the rules were built.** Each decision was first written as a simple countable rule. The decision was then switched back to the app's logic one at a time, to measure what each rule cost in win rate. Rules were revised where the cost was large:
- **Card effects:** a per-card comparison against the app's choices found that Blight, Aquatic, Hibernation, Evolution and Catastrophe were the weakest.
- **Placement:** the Domination-first rule (up to two pawns) is the strongest single rule. One or three pawns were both much worse.
- **Rejected:** more detailed Migration and Speciation rules, Migration group jumps, larger Wanderlust arrivals, an Adaptation dominance rule, and looser Domination triggers. None improved the win rate.

**A shared priority test (tested, rejected).** One test was applied to every action and card choice, looking only at the tiles each option touched, with the existing rule breaking ties. It asked about first place gained, dominance gained, and first place or dominance taken from the leader. At 6 pawns on seed 6200 (360 games), it made the rivals weaker: the scripted player went from 49% to 57%. Adding the terms into one score gave 54%, and dropping the veto on losing first place gave 57%. Judging each choice only by the tiles it touches misses what the per-action rules already capture: species kept spread out for later scoring, cards and Survival.

**Limits.** The scripted player is not a person. Hard varies most between seed blocks. The rules have not been played at a real table.
